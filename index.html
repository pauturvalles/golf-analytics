Key metrics to improveiEleven indicators summarizing the four phases of the game. Ten are pooled (aggregate) values over all holes/shots in the selection — not the mean of a per-round mean — shown as a single-number card; the last one is a 3-line chart with no single figure. The two distance measures (full-swing/short-game proximity) are better the lower they are; the rest of the numeric cards are percentages. Same view selector as the rest of the dashboard. On the ten numeric cards, the large number is the value of the latest visible period in its mini-chart (latest rolling window / month / year, not the mean over the whole selection); it's shown in green when that latest period improves on the previous one, and in red when it worsens — it's not a judgment on whether the value itself is "good," only on its most recent direction of change.

% up & down: of the holes where you miss the green in regulation, how many you finish with at most 1 putt (0 if you chip in) — this measures only the "once I'm close, can I get up and down?" stretch, regardless of how you got there (that's already covered by the approach/tee/OB metrics above). It doesn't require the hole to finish at par or better: a bad tee shot or an earlier penalty doesn't count against you here if you handle the final stretch well. With small samples (3-round window) there may be few missed-green holes in the period — treat that case with caution.

In yearly mode it's shown as grouped bars by type (year A in grey, year B in color) with the same significance marker (●) as the rest of the dashboard.

Strokes Gained by categoryiAll four categories are computed at the individual-shot level, so the Course filter works with exact precision on each one. Methodology, verified shot-by-shot against Shot Scope's own screen: Tee and Approach are split by the tee shot; a non-tee shot counts as Approach if the distance to the hole before the shot is over 45.72m (50 yards), otherwise it's Short Game; a putt counts as Putting only if played from the green — a putt from off the green (a "Texas wedge" from the fringe/fairway) is Short Game. With this rule, the four categories reconstructed here match Shot Scope's exactly, not just their sum.

Data quality caveat: Shot Scope detects putt distance with noticeably less precision than for long shots, and the manual corrections after each round are sometimes eyeballed (e.g., unsure between 2 and 3 meters). That can shift some putts between neighboring buckets — treat the exact detail of any one bucket with a bit less confidence than the aggregate pattern.
